Fast Track '98

A satirical life-simulation strategy game about trying to get ahead without completely forgetting to live.

It is January 1998. Your career is uncertain, tuition costs money, rent keeps showing up, your car has opinions about maintenance, and the local news somehow makes everything worse.

Fast Track '98 is a single-player life-management game inspired by the strategic choices and humor of classic life simulators. Each week gives you limited time and resources. You decide where to go, what to prioritize, what to put off, and what kind of life your character is actually building.

The game mixes serious systems—employment, education, housing, transportation, personal finances, energy, and happiness—with parody businesses, period culture, personalized weekend stories, and a town that reacts to the player's progress.

The gameplay loop

One week. Too many priorities.

Every choice competes for the same limited time, money, and energy. There is no single correct route through the game.

01Choose your character

Start with one of six playable personalities, each bringing a different tone and personal story to the same city.

02Plan your week

Balance work, school, errands, travel, rest, entertainment, and financial obligations before the clock runs out.

03Move through town

Travel across seven districts to reach employers, Knowledge U, stores, services, parks, and entertainment.

04Build a life

Advance your career, earn an education, improve your finances, protect your happiness, and live with the consequences.

Six starting points

Same city. Very different lives.

Current game features.

The preview is already a functioning game, not just a concept demo. Systems will continue to evolve as playtesting and development continue.

Town & travel

Seven-district city

Illustrated town navigation with district destinations, walking, GoBus, bicycle, and multiple vehicle tiers.

Career

Jobs and advancement

Apply through Job Junction, work at employers, pursue raises and promotions, and climb both career and non-career workplace ladders.

Education

Knowledge U

Progress through foundation courses, general education, major pathways, study sessions, and degree milestones that unlock stronger career options.

Money

Financial life

Manage cash, investments, possessions, housing costs, home equity, vehicles, fuel, repairs, arrears, and recovery when money runs out.

Lifestyle

Housing & possessions

Move through multiple housing tiers, buy useful or ridiculous possessions, trade in vehicles, resell items, and improve recovery at home.

Story

1998 satire

CN&N headlines, parody businesses, period references, changing town conditions, and character-driven “Oh, What a Weekend!” stories.

Strategy

Time, energy & happiness

Limited weekly time forces tradeoffs. Faster travel, overtime, study, entertainment, and rest all compete for the same resources.

Scoring

Life Score & high scores

Track Education, Career, Financial, and Happiness performance during the run, then compete against your own local Top-10 results.

Latest preview

v2.1.2

Pre-Social Corrections

This refinement pass tightens district interaction and the job-search experience before the larger Social Life expansion. It preserves the Life Score, financial-recovery, housing, vehicle, workplace, and news systems already established in v2.1.1.

What v2.1.2 corrected

  • Updated clickable district outlines for all five Northside housing scenes plus Center, Eastside, Southeast, and Northeast.
  • Reworked Job Junction so Burger Barn and Taco Bongo remain constant while exactly four non-career openings rotate around them.
  • Made job postings respond to the character’s employment and workplace level, showing appropriate entry-level and next-level opportunities.
  • Restored the first-job safety net: after an initial rejection, the next Burger Barn or Taco Bongo application is guaranteed to succeed.

Systems preserved in this preview

  • Four-pillar Life Score, five-star ratings, local Top-10 high scores, and end-of-campaign score breakdowns.
  • Four-week housing arrears consequences, broke-player recovery, and vehicle fuel/repair disable-and-recovery logic.
  • Workplace ladders, matched Job Junction/employer promotion odds, and the reviewed CN&N story library.
  • Validated browser startup plus expected clickable-location counts across the newly corrected district scenes.

Coming soon

The city is getting more social.

These systems are planned development directions and are not part of the downloadable preview yet.

In design

Social Life

Build friendships and romantic relationships with NPCs across the city. Meet people through activities and locations, maintain relationships through hangouts and dates, and deal with personality compatibility, conflicting priorities, and the occasional spectacularly bad decision.

  • NPC friendships and romance
  • Relationship strength and maintenance
  • Hangouts, dates, and activity choices
  • Happiness and energy effects
  • Relationship-driven Weekend stories
  • A fifth Social Life scoring pillar
Planned

Multiplayer

A future multiplayer layer is planned to bring human competition and comparison into the Fast Track formula. The goal is to let friends build very different lives under the same pressures, then see whose choices actually paid off.

  • Shared competitive life-sim gameplay
  • Human players pursuing different strategies
  • Score and progress comparison
  • Room for rivalry, surprises, and very different definitions of “winning”

Multiplayer mechanics are still in design, so the final format may change as the single-player game is completed and tested.
